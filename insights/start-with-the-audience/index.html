Rethinking Airline Media

Start With the Audience, Not the Ad Space

Advertisers usually know who they want to reach. Airlines create more value when the sales conversation starts there, rather than with a list of available placements.

By Brendan Inns, CEO, Asian Integrated Media
8 November 2026 · 4 min read

Airline media conversations often start in the wrong place.

The airline opens the rate card and explains what is available: a magazine page, an inflight entertainment spot, a website banner or perhaps a lounge opportunity.

The advertiser is then expected to decide which format to buy.

But that usually isn't the question the advertiser is trying to answer. The real question is much simpler: can this airline help us reach the right people, in the right setting, at the right time?

Begin with the advertiser's question

A luxury brand may want to reach affluent international travellers. A bank may be looking for business owners and senior decision-makers. A tourism board may want passengers who are considering a particular destination.

These are audience questions, not format questions.

Once the audience and objective are clear, the media choices become easier. The right answer might be a premium-cabin campaign, an activation in the lounge, destination content, inflight Wi-Fi or a combination of several touchpoints.

Starting with the available space reverses that process. It asks the advertiser to fit its objective into the airline's inventory, rather than using the airline's strengths to solve the advertiser's problem.

Premium isn't one audience

Airlines often describe their passengers as a premium audience. That can be true, but it doesn't tell an advertiser enough.

A First Class passenger flying to London may have very different interests from a frequent regional business traveller or a family taking a long-haul holiday. They may all be valuable, but they are valuable for different reasons.

Cabin, route, destination, travel frequency, loyalty status and stage of the journey can all help explain who the audience is and why it matters.

This doesn’t mean dividing passengers into dozens of complicated groups. It means helping advertisers understand who they can reach, where those passengers are travelling and why they are relevant to the campaign. A total passenger count or a general description such as “affluent travellers” tells only part of that story.

Context can matter as much as reach

Audience size matters, but the setting in which a brand appears can be just as important.

A watch brand displayed in a First Class lounge is not simply reaching a number of people. It is appearing in a calm, carefully managed environment that supports the brand's position.

A destination message shown while someone is planning a trip has a different role from the same message shown after the passenger has arrived. An inflight Wi-Fi offer can be useful at one point in the journey and irrelevant at another.

Context also includes brand safety. Airline-managed media gives brands access to a controlled environment, with oversight of the content and advertising passengers encounter. For advertisers, that control is part of the value, alongside the audience itself and the moment in which the message appears.

Good airline media planning brings the audience, setting and timing together. That is what turns access to passengers into a meaningful advertising opportunity.

Useful insight doesn't require personal data

Airlines sometimes assume that better audience planning requires sharing personal passenger information. It doesn't.

Advertisers do not need passenger names. They need enough reliable, aggregated information to understand who they can reach and whether the audience fits their campaign.

This may include broad passenger profiles, cabin and route information, relevant travel behaviour and the likely context in which the message will appear.

The aim is not to collect data for its own sake. It is to make the media opportunity easier to understand, easier to plan and easier to justify.

A better brief leads to better ideas

When a sales conversation begins with the audience and the advertiser's objective, it opens the door to better ideas.

A skincare brand may be interested in product discovery and trial. A hotel group may want to reach passengers before they choose where to stay. A financial brand may want a trusted environment in which to speak to frequent business travellers.

Each brief points towards a different answer. One may call for a lounge experience supported by digital content. Another may work best through destination media or inflight connectivity. A third may need a longer-term presence across several routes and channels.

That is a more useful conversation than asking every advertiser to choose from the same menu.

The commercial value follows

An audience-led approach is better for advertisers, but it also makes commercial sense for airlines.

Relevant campaigns are easier to sell, easier for passengers to accept and more likely to grow into larger partnerships. They also make it possible to price the opportunity around its real value, rather than treating every placement as a standard unit of space.

Airlines already know a great deal about their passengers and the journeys they take. The opportunity is to turn that understanding into media propositions that advertisers can use.

The strongest airline media conversations don't begin with what is available. They begin with who the advertiser needs to reach and work forward from there.
