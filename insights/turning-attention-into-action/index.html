Rethinking Airline Media

Turning Passenger Attention Into Action

Reaching the passenger is only the first step. The stronger opportunity is to offer a useful and natural next action, without turning every part of the journey into a sales pitch.

By Brendan Inns, CEO, Asian Integrated Media
8 November 2026 · 5 min read

Airlines offer advertisers something increasingly difficult to find: the attention of a valuable audience in a trusted environment.

That attention matters. But attention on its own is not always enough.

A passenger may notice a product, watch a piece of content or spend time with a brand in the lounge. The next question is what they can do if they are interested.

Too often, the campaign ends just as the passenger's interest begins.

Attention is the beginning

Traditional advertising has often treated exposure as the final result. The message appeared, the passenger saw it and the campaign was delivered.

Exposure still has value, particularly for premium brands building awareness and association. Not every campaign should be judged by an immediate sale.

But when a passenger does want to learn more, try a product, make a booking or visit a store, the opportunity should not disappear.

The airline journey provides several natural ways to continue that engagement. The important word is natural.

Give passengers a useful next step

A next step should make sense for the passenger, the brand and the stage of the journey.

Someone trying a skincare product in a lounge might want to order it for home delivery. A passenger watching destination content may want to save an itinerary. Someone using sponsored inflight Wi-Fi might value an offer connected with where they are travelling.

The action does not have to be a purchase. It might be reading more, joining a programme, requesting information, visiting a location or saving something for later.

The best next step is often the simplest one. If passengers need to search for the brand, remember a complicated address or repeat information they have already entered, much of the interest will be lost.

Commerce is wider than onboard shopping

Airline commerce is often discussed as onboard retail. That remains important, but the wider opportunity is much larger.

Airlines already connect passengers with hotels, destinations, restaurants, transport, financial services, entertainment and shopping. Advertising can help passengers discover these services at useful points in the journey.

A hotel group can reach travellers before they choose where to stay. A tourism partner can suggest activities before arrival. A luxury brand can connect a lounge experience with a store visit at the destination.

In each case, the media creates awareness and the connected service gives the passenger somewhere relevant to go next.

Timing can strengthen relevance

A message may be relevant because of the passenger’s interests, lifestyle or professional needs. Timing can make it more useful or easier to act on.

An airport transfer offer has an obvious connection to arrival. A watch, skincare or financial services message may remain relevant throughout the journey because of who the passenger is.

The opportunity is to consider when someone is most likely to notice, explore or respond. A lounge experience might encourage product discovery. Inflight content can build understanding. A follow-up invitation can make it easier to take the next step.

Repetition also has a role. Seeing a consistent message across several touchpoints can reinforce recognition and recall. Good planning determines when to repeat the message, when to develop it and when to introduce a clear opportunity to act.

Make the channels work together

An omnichannel approach connects the relevant channels around the audience and campaign objective. Each touchpoint should reinforce the brand and make it easier for interested passengers to continue engaging, whether they encounter the campaign in a lounge, through inflight media or online.

Digital media can introduce the idea and make it easy to respond. The lounge can provide time and space for discovery. Inflight media can deepen the story. Connectivity can offer a direct next step. Destination activity can turn interest into a physical visit or experience.

This does not require every campaign to use every channel. It requires the selected channels to support one another rather than operate as unrelated placements.

When the pieces connect, the advertiser gains a clearer path from awareness to engagement and action. The passenger receives a more useful and consistent experience.

Measure the action that matters

Different campaigns should lead to different measures.

A brand-awareness campaign may focus on reach, attention and recall. A content partnership may look at views and engagement. A retail campaign may measure product interaction, website visits, enquiries or sales.

The mistake is expecting one measure to explain every campaign.

Agreeing on the desired action at the beginning makes the campaign easier to plan and the results easier to understand. It also gives the airline and advertiser something useful to learn for the next campaign.

Trust comes first

Connecting media with action should never mean pressuring passengers or using their information carelessly.

The passenger should understand what is being offered and choose whether to engage. Any use of data should be clear, appropriate and handled within the airline's privacy standards.

This matters commercially as well as ethically. The trust passengers place in an airline is part of what makes the environment valuable to advertisers.

A short-term sale is not worth weakening that trust.

A more complete opportunity

Airline media should still create awareness, build brands and tell stories. Those roles remain important.

But where there is a natural opportunity for passengers to continue the journey with a brand, the connection should be easy to make.

That may lead to a purchase. It may lead to a visit, a booking, an enquiry or simply a deeper level of interest.

The point is not to turn every passenger moment into a transaction. It is to make sure that when attention becomes interest, there is somewhere useful for that interest to go.
