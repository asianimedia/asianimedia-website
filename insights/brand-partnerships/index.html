Rethinking Airline Media

The Best Brand Partnerships Give Passengers More

The strongest airline partnerships do more than place a brand in front of passengers. They connect with their interests, needs or aspirations through something useful, relevant or memorable.

By Brendan Inns, CEO, Asian Integrated Media
8 November 2026 · 5 min read

Airline media gives brands access to valuable audiences in carefully managed environments. A well-placed message can be highly effective because it speaks to the people travelling.

A prominent logo and valuable media space can build awareness. A deeper partnership gives passengers a reason to engage with the brand, whether through their interests, lifestyle, professional needs or the journey itself.

The best partnerships build on that relevance. They offer something the passenger can enjoy, use, discover or remember, with an execution that suits the setting.

That doesn't make the partnership less commercial. In many cases, it makes it more valuable.

Begin with the passenger

A strong airline partnership should begin with a simple question: why would this matter to the passenger?

The answer might be a personal interest, a professional need, a lifestyle aspiration or something that improves the travel experience. It could be discovering a watch, exploring a financial service, trying a skincare product or enjoying a better lounge experience.

Brand exposure is a valid objective. A partnership can build on that exposure by giving the right audience a meaningful way to engage.

This doesn’t mean every activation has to be elaborate or travel-related. Thoughtful content or an opportunity to discover a product can be just as effective as a large physical installation. What matters is that the idea is relevant to the passenger and presented appropriately in the setting.

Relevance makes a brand welcome

Premium airline environments are carefully managed. Passengers expect the cabin and lounge experience to feel calm, considered and consistent with the airline's brand.

Relevance can come from the journey, but it can also come from who the passenger is. A watch partnership may appeal to an interest in craftsmanship. A financial brand may speak to business owners or investors. Neither needs a travel theme to be relevant. A skincare experience in a lounge, a destination offer before arrival or sponsored connectivity can also respond to needs during the trip.

Audience fit and execution both matter. Even a highly relevant brand needs an approach that respects the cabin or lounge environment.

Good curation brings the brand, audience and setting together. It protects the passenger experience while giving the advertiser a credible context in which to engage.

Bring the brand to life through experience

Some products are difficult to understand through a screen or printed page alone.

Skincare needs to be touched and tried. A premium drink is better understood when it is tasted. The craftsmanship of a watch or luxury product can become clearer when passengers see it at close range.

Airline lounges can offer brands a rare opportunity to create that physical experience for an audience with time to engage.

Over the years, AIM has helped develop airline partnerships involving brands such as La Mer, Omega, Cartier and Mandarin Oriental. The ideas ranged from lounge experiences and seasonal installations to premium dining and longer-term media partnerships.

The strongest of these worked because the experience, the audience and the brand fitted together. The advertising supported the idea, rather than being the entire idea.

Connect the right touchpoints

A brand might pair an advertisement on the inflight entertainment system with clearly labelled branded content in the inflight magazine. The screen introduces the brand and builds recognition; the magazine gives interested passengers a deeper story to explore. A watch brand, for example, could use film to convey its design and craftsmanship, with a magazine feature exploring the people and expertise behind the product. The connection comes from the passenger’s interests, while each format contributes something different.

A lounge activation can become more valuable when it connects with relevant media before or after the visit. The choice of touchpoints should follow the audience and campaign objective.

A partnership might combine digital content before departure, a physical experience in the lounge and related inflight media. An invitation to visit a store, explore a service or continue engaging online can extend the relationship beyond the flight. The brand’s relevance may remain rooted in the passenger’s interests throughout.

That doesn't mean repeating the same message everywhere. Each part should play a different role while supporting the same idea.

This gives the passenger a more natural experience and gives the advertiser more than a single moment of exposure. It also makes the partnership easier to understand and measure as a whole.

Protect the environment

There is always a temptation to add more branding when a commercial partner is involved. More logos, more signs and more messages can seem like greater value.

In premium environments, the opposite is often true.

Restraint can make a partnership feel more confident. The right materials, service, content and staff interaction may carry the brand more effectively than a wall of logos.

Airlines should be willing to say no to ideas that do not fit. Protecting the environment is not a barrier to revenue. It is what preserves the value of the opportunity in the first place.

The commercial value follows

When a partnership works for passengers, it is more likely to work for the advertiser and the airline as well.

Passengers have more reasons to engage. Brands can build stronger relationships with a relevant audience, while airlines can develop larger and longer-term commercial partnerships.

A good partnership may also support other parts of the airline's business, including retail, dining, connectivity, loyalty or destination activity.

The aim is to create a commercial idea that earns the passenger’s interest, through its relevance to their life, their work or their journey.

That is when a brand partnership stops feeling like an interruption and starts feeling like part of the experience.
