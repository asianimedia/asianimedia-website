Rethinking Airline Media

Better Measurement Makes Airline Media More Valuable

Airline media has valuable audiences and distinctive environments. Clear objectives, sensible measures and honest reporting help advertisers understand that value and invest with greater confidence.

By Brendan Inns, CEO, Asian Integrated Media
8 November 2026 · 4 min read

Airline media has always had a strong story to tell.

Passengers are attentive. The environment is trusted. Premium cabins and lounges offer brands access to audiences that can be difficult to reach elsewhere.

But advertisers increasingly want more than a strong story. They want to understand what their investment delivered.

That does not mean every campaign needs perfect data or an immediate sales result. It means the airline and advertiser should agree on what success looks like and report it as clearly as possible.

Decide what success means first

Measurement should begin before the campaign, not after it.

If the aim is awareness, the advertiser may care about reach, attention and recall. If the campaign is designed to encourage product discovery, interaction may matter more. If there is a direct next action, website visits, enquiries, bookings or sales may be relevant.

Without a clear objective, the post-campaign report can become a collection of numbers with no real conclusion.

A simple question at the beginning can prevent that: what should passengers think, feel or do after seeing this campaign?

One measure cannot explain everything

A magazine page, a lounge experience, an inflight entertainment placement and a digital campaign do different jobs. They should not all be judged in the same way.

Digital media can often report impressions, clicks and engagement. Inflight connectivity may show sessions or interactions. A lounge activation may measure participation, product trials, enquiries or related retail activity.

Other formats may be better suited to audience delivery, attention, brand recall or association.

The measure should fit the role of the channel. Forcing every format into the same reporting model can make useful media look weak and ordinary digital activity look more meaningful than it really is.

Do not confuse clicks with value

Clicks are easy to count, which is one reason they receive so much attention. But a click is not always the most important result.

A passenger may spend time with a brand in a lounge, remember an inflight message or develop a stronger view of a product without clicking anything.

Premium brands often care about context, attention and association as well as immediate response. These outcomes can be harder to measure, but they should not be ignored simply because a digital number is easier to produce.

Good measurement uses the available evidence without pretending that one number tells the whole story.

Measure what can be measured

Airline media does not need to wait for a perfect measurement system before improving its reporting.

Basic information can already make a significant difference: where and when the campaign ran, the audience it was expected to reach, how many passengers had access to it and what engagement took place.

For connected campaigns, the airline can also look at how the different parts worked together. Did the digital content lead passengers to the lounge experience? Did product trial support retail interest? Did sponsored connectivity generate meaningful engagement?

The answer may not always be complete. What matters is being clear about what is known, what is estimated and what cannot yet be measured.

Reporting should be easy to understand

A campaign report should help the advertiser understand what happened. It should not require them to decode internal airline systems or media terminology.

The clearest reports usually answer a small number of questions: what ran, where did it run, who could see it, what response was recorded and what did we learn?

Results should be presented honestly, including any limitations. If part of the audience figure is estimated, say so. If tracking was unavailable for one element, explain that rather than filling the gap with a vague claim.

Straightforward reporting builds trust, even when the results are not perfect.

Use the results to improve

Measurement is not only about proving that the campaign worked. It should help make the next campaign better.

The results may show that one route responded more strongly, that passengers spent more time with one type of content or that a lounge experience worked best at a particular time of day.

Those lessons can improve the audience, timing, message and channel mix next time. They can also help the airline develop stronger media products and more useful packages.

A report that simply confirms delivery closes the campaign. A report that identifies what was learned helps begin the next one.

Better evidence creates confidence

Advertisers do not expect every airline media channel to behave like an online advertisement. They do expect the value of the opportunity to be explained and supported.

Clear objectives, appropriate measures and transparent reporting make it easier for advertisers to compare options, defend their investment and return with larger briefs.

This is not about reducing airline media to a spreadsheet. The quality of the audience, the setting and the passenger experience will always matter.

Better measurement helps those strengths receive the credit they deserve.
